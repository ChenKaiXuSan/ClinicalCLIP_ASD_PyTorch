Prior-Defined Gait Measurements across Three Disorders}

\begin{abstract}
Clinical knowledge about gait---which body regions an expert watches, which signs a rating
scale names---is usually given to video models as supervision. We study the alternative of
letting that knowledge decide \emph{what to measure}: a prior, stated once and offline, names
a handful of kinematic quantities, which are computed geometrically from estimated 3D
keypoints and classified by an untuned logistic regression. On lateral gait videos of adult
spinal deformity (67 patients, regions marked by two clinicians), five such quantities reach
$\asdPriorTwo$ balanced accuracy where a Kinetics-pretrained 3D CNN, a video transformer and
a region-grounded network reach $\asdVidLo$--$\asdVidHi$ and four skeleton networks are at
chance. The recipe transfers unchanged to two public datasets: eight quantities named by the
MDS-UPDRS gait item average $\pdMeanTwo$ macro-F1 over the four CARE-PD cohorts under the
official protocol, against $\pdBestEncTwo$ for the best of seven pretrained motion encoders,
and twelve quantities reach $\koaPriorTwo$ on KOA-PD-NM against $\koaVideoTwo$ for a video
network once background shortcuts are removed. A language model given only the prior text
proposes measurement sets close to the hand-written ones. Finally, fusing the measurements
with a second branch by an evidence-strength gate helps when that branch reads pixels
($\asdFusMeasTwo\!\to\!\asdGateTwo$ on \asd) and not when it reads the same keypoints. The
gains over learned models are modest, and we report the controls that bound them.
\keywords{Clinical priors \and Gait analysis \and Interpretability \and Small data.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A clinician assessing gait does not look everywhere. For adult spinal deformity (\asd) the
eye goes to the trunk, pelvis and head~\cite{glassman2005sagittal,schwab2012srs}; for
Parkinson's disease the MDS-UPDRS tells the rater to judge stride amplitude, foot lift, heel
strike and arm swing~\cite{goetz2008mdsupdrs}. Video-based assessment needs no markers, and
the usual way to exploit such knowledge is to make a network \emph{learn from} it: a
grounding loss on its attention, concept supervision, or an auxiliary modality aligned with
the video~\cite{koh2020cbm,radford2021clip}.

We argue that this misreads what the knowledge is. ``Watch the trunk and the head'' is not a
label for a patient; it is a statement about which quantities carry the diagnosis. We
therefore use a clinical prior for one thing only, offline: to choose a small set of named
kinematic measurements, computed geometrically from estimated 3D keypoints and classified
by an untuned logistic regression; the prior is never needed at test time
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

We test the idea on three datasets under their own protocols. \textbf{(i)} On a private
\asd\ cohort with region annotations from two clinicians, using the annotation as supervision
buys a checkable attention map but no accuracy, whereas five quantities defined by the
marked regions outperform every end-to-end model we trained. \textbf{(ii)} The same pipeline,
with the prior replaced by a public rating rubric, is on par with or above pretrained motion
encoders on the four labelled cohorts of CARE-PD~\cite{carepd2025} and above
shortcut-controlled video and skeleton networks on KOA-PD-NM~\cite{kour2022koapdnm_icicc}.
\textbf{(iii)} The step from prior to measurement set can be delegated to a language model
that sees only text. \textbf{(iv)} A per-patient \emph{evidence-strength gate} that defers to
a second branch when the measurements are unremarkable adds accuracy when the second branch
reads pixels; when it reads the same keypoints it adds nothing, on all three datasets.
Measurement sets are fixed before outcomes are seen and we report random-set, permutation
and shortcut controls; as cohorts are small and most differences lie within bootstrap
intervals, we claim parity or better with a readable decision, not superiority.

\section{Related Work}

Concept bottlenecks~\cite{koh2020cbm} and attention supervision align a network with
expert concepts or regions, typically buying an explanation at unchanged accuracy; post-hoc
saliency~\cite{selvaraju2017gradcam} need not recover what experts attend to. The learned
alternatives are Kinetics-pretrained video
networks~\cite{feichtenhofer2019slowfast,bertasius2021timesformer}, skeleton graph
networks~\cite{yan2018stgcn,chen2021ctrgcn}, frozen motion encoders with linear
probes~\cite{carepd2025}, and pose as a clinically motivated \emph{input} to screening
networks~\cite{zhou2024gaitpatterns,zhou2025poseprior}. Hand-crafted gait parameters are
older than all of these; what we add is a test of whether a stated prior, mapped to a few
measurements \emph{before} seeing data, holds up against such models under matched
protocols, whether the mapping can be automated, and when the two are complementary. The
fusion rule is a gated variant of probability-level
combination~\cite{kittler1998combining}, interpretable by design~\cite{rudin2019stop}.

\subsection{From a clinical prior to a measurement set}
\label{sec:prior}

A \emph{prior} $\Pi$ is whatever clinicians have already written down about a disorder's
gait. We use three kinds. For \asd, two spine surgeons each chose, for two clips
of every recording ($\annClips$ clips, $\annPatients$ patients), the one region among five
they attend to; at least one chose lumbar--pelvis for $\annLumbar$\% of clips, head for
$\annHead$\% and shoulder for $\annShoulder$\% (wrist $\annWrist$\%, foot $\annFoot$\%), and
they agree on only $\annAgree$\%, so we read the annotation as a group-level prior
$\Pi_{\mathrm{ASD}}{=}\{$lumbar--pelvis, head, shoulder$\}$. For Parkinson's disease
$\Pi_{\mathrm{PD}}$ is the text of MDS-UPDRS item 3.10~\cite{goetz2008mdsupdrs} (``stride
amplitude, stride speed, height of foot lift, heel strike during walking, turning, and arm
swing''). For knee osteoarthritis $\Pi_{\mathrm{KOA}}$ is reduced knee
excursion~\cite{mills2013koagait,boekesteijn2022imukoa}, to which we added side-to-side
asymmetry and longer double support, expected for an often unilateral, painful disorder.

Measurements are expressed in a fixed grammar $\mathcal{G}$ over body landmarks in a
walking-aligned frame: \emph{posture} quantities (sagittal inclination, offset or distance
of a landmark pair, or a joint angle, summarised over 1-s segments by mean, extreme or
range) and \emph{per-step} quantities computed from foot contacts, detected as extrema of
the fore--aft inter-ankle distance~\cite{zeni2008gait} (step length, cadence, speed, joint
ranges, foot lift, foot pitch at contact, arm-swing excursion, asymmetries, double support).
A measurement set is a subset $S(\Pi)\subset\mathcal{G}$ fixed before any outcome is seen.
$S(\Pi_{\mathrm{ASD}})$ has five posture quantities at the three regions: trunk lean,
shoulder offset, head-forward angle, maximum hip flexion and hip range.
$S(\Pi_{\mathrm{PD}})$ has the eight per-step quantities the item names: step length,
speed, cadence, ankle and toe lift, foot pitch at contact, and arm-swing excursion (mean of
the two sides and the smaller side). $S(\Pi_{\mathrm{KOA}})$ adds four: knee range and its
asymmetry, step-length asymmetry and double-support fraction. The mapping $\Pi\mapsto S$ is made either by us or by a language model that receives the
prior text, the landmark list and $\mathcal{G}$ and returns $K$ grammar items; it sees no
video, keypoint or label. As controls we draw random sets of the same size from
$\mathcal{G}$, withhold the prior from the language model, and let data-driven selectors
($\ell_1$ regression, boosting) choose from the whole grammar.

\subsection{Measurement branch}

A subject is the mean of each quantity over their segments or walks,
$\mathbf{m}_i\in\mathbb{R}^{|S|}$. With $\boldsymbol\mu,\boldsymbol\sigma$ from the training
subjects, $\mathbf{z}_i=(\mathbf{m}_i-\boldsymbol\mu)/\boldsymbol\sigma$ is classified by a
class-balanced (multinomial) logistic regression with default regularisation ($C{=}1$),
giving $P^{m}_i$. Nothing is tuned on the outcome; the model has $|S|{+}1$ parameters per
class.

\subsection{Evidence-strength gating}
\label{sec:gate}

A measurement vector near the population norm says little; a strongly deviating one is
decisive. We quantify this by the \emph{deviation}
$d_i=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{j}|z_{ij}|$, the subject's mean absolute $z$-score under
training-fold statistics, and fuse $P^{m}_i$ with the output $P^{v}_i$ of a second branch as
\begin{equation}
P_i=w_i\,P^{m}_i+(1-w_i)\,P^{v}_i,\qquad
w_i=\begin{cases} w_{\mathrm{lo}} & d_i<\tau\\ w_{\mathrm{hi}} & d_i\ge\tau\end{cases},
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
with $\tau$ the $q$-quantile of the training deviations. The triple
$(q,w_{\mathrm{lo}},w_{\mathrm{hi}})$ is chosen from a small grid
($q\in\{0.2,\ldots,0.5\}$, $w_{\mathrm{lo}}\in\{0,0.25,0.5\}$, $w_{\mathrm{hi}}\in\{0.75,1\}$)
on the out-of-fold predictions of the other folds by balanced accuracy; the test fold only
applies the rule. Under the same nesting we compare a fixed $w{=}0.5$, a nested global $w$,
and the gate with $d$ permuted across subjects, which keeps the rule's flexibility but
removes its information.

\paragraph{Second branch and contrast models.} The second branch is a Kinetics-pretrained
slow\_r50~\cite{feichtenhofer2019slowfast} fine-tuned on 1-s segments of 8 frames; to test
what the gate needs we replace it by ST-GCN~\cite{yan2018stgcn} on the same keypoints the
measurements use. As the conventional use of the \asd\ prior we train a region-grounded
variant: concept queries per region over the slow\_r50 token grid, a presence head, and a
KL loss tying each attention map to the region rendered on the skeleton.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets and protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\textbf{\asd} (private; collected at **** with ethics approval ****). Lateral walking videos
of 79 patients; 2D keypoints from YOLOv8~\cite{ultralytics2023yolov8}, 3D keypoints from
SAM 3D Body~\cite{sam3dbody2025} exist for 67 (46 \asd, 21 non-\asd: dropped-head syndrome,
lumbar stenosis, hip osteoarthritis), the cohort used throughout. End-to-end models share
one nested five-fold patient split (validation selects the checkpoint), 50 epochs and five
seeds; measurement models have no training randomness, so their five-fold partition is
redrawn ten times. We report patient-level balanced accuracy (chance $0.5$) and AUC.
\textbf{CARE-PD}~\cite{carepd2025} (public, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). SMPL gait sequences with clinician-rated
UPDRS-gait scores per walk for four cohorts: PD-GaM (30 subjects, 1,700 walks), BMCLab (23,
781), T-SDU-PD (14, 381), 3DGait (43, 90). We follow the official protocol: within-cohort
leave-one-subject-out, walk-level predictions pooled over folds, macro-F1 over scores 0--2.
Encoder results are quoted from the benchmark paper (frozen encoder, linear probe, one run),
not re-run. \textbf{KOA-PD-NM}~\cite{kour2022koapdnm_icicc,kour2020koapdnm} (public, CC BY 4.0). Sagittal RGB videos of 50
knee-osteoarthritis, 16 Parkinson's and 30 healthy subjects, two walks each (190 usable
videos). Keypoints, splits and video model are as for \asd\ (with a class-weighted loss); the
metric is three-class balanced accuracy. Acquisition differs with the label here---frame rate (98\% of KOA clips
at 50\,fps, 86\% of healthy clips at 30), room, clothing---so all videos are resampled by
time to 25\,fps and the video branch sees person-centred crops with the background removed
by instance segmentation (YOLOv8); raw-frame and silhouette-only variants serve as checks.

Table~\ref{tab:asd} gives the comparison. The video models reach $\asdVidLo$--$\asdVidHi$;
grounding the 3D CNN to the clinician regions yields attention that matches the annotation
($\asdAlign$ alignment against $\asdGradcam$ for Grad-CAM on the plain network) but $\asdGrounded$
accuracy, no better than the ungrounded $\asdVideo$. The four skeleton networks are at
chance ($\asdSkelLo$--$\asdSkelHi$). The five prior-defined quantities reach
$\asdPrior\pm\asdPriorSd$ (AUC $\asdPriorAuc$), and $\asdPriorTwoD$ from 2D keypoints, so the
result does not hinge on the 3D estimator. The same 3D joints that are uninformative through
a graph network are informative as five numbers: with 67 patients the bottleneck is knowing
what to compute, not capacity. Four plausible quantities at unmarked regions (knee flexion,
step length, speed, arm swing) give $\asdUnmarked$, and adding them to the five lowers
accuracy to $\asdNine$. Trunk lean alone gives $\asdTrunk$; the head-forward angle alone is uninformative
($\asdHeadAlone$) yet removing it costs the most ($\asdMinusHead$): given the trunk
inclination, it separates forward-inclined \asd\ from dropped-head non-\asd\ patients.

\subsection{Is it the selection, and can it be automated?}

On \asd, the five prior quantities written in the grammar (hips left and right apart,
$\asdRandK$ columns, $\asdPriorBank$) lie at percentile $\asdRandAllPct$ of $\asdRandN$ random
sets of equal size ($\asdRandClinPct$ when drawn within the clinician regions,
$\asdRandUnmPct$ within unmarked ones); data-driven selection over all 1,272 candidates,
which sees the labels, is on par with the five as defined ($\ell_1$: $\asdLone$, AUC
$\asdLoneAuc$; boosting: $\asdGb$, $\asdGbAuc$; prior: $\asdPrior$, $\asdPriorAuc$). On CARE-PD
the rubric set lies at percentiles $\pdGamPct$, $\pdBmcPct$, $\pdTsduPct$ and $\pdTdgPct$ of
random per-step sets, on KOA-PD-NM at $\koaRandPct$. A prior thus beats most label-free
choices without being exceptional: clearly on PD-GaM, 3DGait and KOA-PD-NM, moderately on
\asd, not on BMCLab or T-SDU-PD, where impairment is global and most kinematic quantities
track severity. Given the item text, two open language models (Qwen3-32B~\cite{qwen3report2025},
gpt-oss-20b~\cite{gptoss2025}; 20 samples each) propose sets averaging $\pdLlmRubric$ on CARE-PD against $\pdMean$ for ours,
$\pdLlmNoRubric$ when the text is withheld and $\pdRand$ for random sets
(Table~\ref{tab:public}). On \asd, sets proposed from the clinician regions score
$\asdLlmMarked$, as do the prior five in the grammar ($\asdPriorBank$) and, notably, sets
proposed from the disease name alone ($\asdLlmNoRegion$); sets for unmarked regions score
$\asdLlmUnmarked$. The model's own knowledge of the disease is thus a usable prior.

On \asd\ (Table~\ref{tab:fusion}) the measurement branch scores $\asdFusMeas$ on the fixed

above the permuted-deviation gate ($\asdPerm$) in $\asdGateGtPerm$\% of permutations.
Fig.~\ref{fig:strata} shows why: in the tercile of least deviation, $\asdStrLowAsd$\% of
patients are \asd---patients without visible sagittal compensation---and the measurement
branch is right $\asdStrLowMeas$ of the time against $\asdStrLowVideo$ for the video; in the
top tercile the measurements are right $\asdStrHighMeas$ of the time. With $d$ from the four
unmarked-region quantities the gate gives $\asdGateUnmDev$.

Replacing the video by a skeleton network removes the gain: with ST-GCN on the 3D joints
the gate gives $\asdGateSkelThree$ ($\asdGateSkelThreeDelta$ against the measurements). On
\asd\ this may only reflect a weak skeleton network; KOA-PD-NM separates the two
explanations. In a secondary, post-hoc analysis in the binary form of the \asd\ task
(disorder versus healthy), the background-removed video branch scores $\koaVideoBin$,
the measurements $\koaMeasBin$ and the gate $\koaGateBin$ (permuted $\koaPermBin$, beaten in
$\koaGateGtPermBin$\% of permutations), whereas ST-GCN alone scores
$\koaSkelBin$---as good as the video---yet gating with it gives $\koaGateSkelBin$: its
errors coincide with those of the measurements (either-correct bound $\koaEitherSkelBin$
against $\koaEitherVideoBin$ for video). In the pre-specified three-class task the pattern
is the same in direction but weaker: fusion with video reaches $\koaGateThree$ from
$\koaMeasThree$, the permuted gate $\koaPermThree$ and plain averaging $\koaFixedThree$, so
the benefit there is that of fusion rather than of gating; with ST-GCN it is
$\koaGateSkelThree$. CARE-PD releases no video, and with a joint-based second branch no
fusion rule helps. Measurements and learned features are complementary when the learner sees what keypoints
discard, and the deviation routes well when the measurements have a blind sub-population.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

A clinical prior is coarse as a label and precise as a pointer: as supervision it taught a
network where to look, not what the look means; as a specification it turned three short
statements into five, eight and twelve numbers that a linear model exploits and a reader can
check. \emph{Limits.} Cohorts have 14--96 subjects and intervals are wide; on the public
data we claim parity with far simpler models, not a significant gain. The PD set was our second; the KOA-PD-NM binary analysis was
post-hoc and its groups differ in age, sex and clothing; gate gains amount to one to five
subjects and need a second branch with complementary errors; the \asd\ data come from one
centre. Within these limits, across three disorders, clinical knowledge
is most useful not as something a gait network should learn but as a specification of
what to measure.
